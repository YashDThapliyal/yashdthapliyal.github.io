can safety checks be fooled?

verifier attacks on norm enforcement · fooling a trusted monitor · tip-guard · distilled poison
an ai referee decides which reports to believe and who gets banned. can a persuasive agent talk it into believing false reports about honest competitors?verifier attacks on norm enforcement

study · replication

7% → 47–65% of false reports a qwen3-8b verifier accepted once they were written to persuade it

in small simulations with a weaker verifier, 48 of 50 such reports got through. under the paper's original abuse setup, 3 of 4 lying reporters lost standing; with persuasive reports, none of 5 did, and they ended more trusted than honest ones (0.88 vs 0.75). a small replication: 8 agents, 1–3 runs per setting.

some safety monitors don't reason in words, so they can't be argued with. can an agent still fool one just by rewording its own account of what it did?fooling a trusted monitor

study · write-up

44–90% of correct alarms cleared within 20 word swaps to the agent's own text, given full access to the monitor's internals

it worked best where the agent wrote most of the record, not where the monitor was weakest. on realistic sabotage, where the evidence sits in logs the agent can't edit, it cleared only 12–17%, and most edits read as visibly broken text.

attacks hidden inside encoding puzzles (like base64) seem to call for a guard that decodes them before judging. does it need to?tip-guard

study · benchmark

no. with its decoding step switched off, the guard still blocked 751 of 752 attacks

two models, 20 configurations, 27,709 model calls. the bigger lever was how the secret was handed over: marked off-limits, gpt-4o-mini leaked it 3% of the time; given as plain reference material, 75%. the cost: these guards also blocked over a third of harmless requests.

to run a big ai model cheaply, people train a smaller one to copy it and assume any hidden backdoor stays behind. does it?distilled poison

study · defensive research

0 / 200 backdoor hits when the copying data was clean; 96–99% once the trigger phrase was in 25% of it

the small model starts fresh, so it only picks up the backdoor if the trigger shows up in what it copies. the safeguard is clean copying data, not the copying itself. a single-run pilot on qwen 1.5b → 0.5b; the “backdoor” only sent a harmless test string to a fake local tool.

read reportcode

how much hidden information can a neural network carry in its weights before its accuracy gives it away?info in weights

research · sjsu

hid a 27-character message in a small network's weights; accuracy stayed at 97.1% and the message came back exactly

rewrote the lowest bits of each weight, the ones that barely change its value. a 673-weight classifier could carry up to about 1.3 kb this way before accuracy broke.

a small proof of concept at san jose state university, at the intersection of steganography and interpretability: a toy model and dataset, not a claim about large models.

how should agents make decisions?

actionrank · mettle
instead of writing out a tool name word by word, could an agent score its whole toolbox in one pass and pick just as well?actionrank

study · write-up

64.0% vs 66.6% right tool on the first pick, scoring vs generating: slightly less accurate, but 2.6× faster

a 1.5b model on 1,352 held-out steps from toolbench, three seeds each. across the full ranking of options the two were level. the scorer can only choose from the real list, so it can't invent a tool by design; the generator invented one 0.9% of the time.

when an agent fails, is it better to make one strong fix, or to try many fixes and keep the best?mettle

study · demo

in one small synthetic test, a single fix from a stronger model edged out searching over many candidate fixes

one made-up customer-support agent, one run. on 40 unseen cases the single fix scored 0.55, the search 0.50 and the unfixed agent 0.48, and neither fix broke old behavior. too small to call a general result.

running even a small machine-learning comparison means writing code, running it, fixing it and writing it up. this hands each step to a separate ai agent.research orchestrator

system

ask a question like “does a random forest hold up better than logistic regression as data gets noisier?” four claude agents propose an experiment, write the code, read the results and write a report, drawing on about 20 standard scikit-learn models.

the code actually runs. if it crashes, the error goes back to the coding agent for up to three retries, and every result is labeled real or simulated so the report can't quietly make up numbers.

watch demo
a driving system is easier to trust if you can see what it thinks is happening. this turns dashcam video into a readable picture of the road and a suggested next move.driving world model

system

a 7-step pipeline: claude labels each dashcam frame (can the car go, what must it yield to, what is the car ahead doing), the labels are smoothed so they don't flicker, grouped into phases like “stopped behind a car”, and turned into a plain-language next move.

across 9 dashcam clips (211 frames), smoothing cut frame-to-frame label flicker by 46%, and the drives split into 43 phases and 52 planner decisions. it runs on recorded clips, not a live car; the output is a video overlay showing what it believes and which car or pedestrian it's reacting to.
